Goals ÷ Time = Tension

A life formula for anyone carrying too many backpacks

Goals ÷ Time = Tension

Not a scientific formula. A life formula.

Most people think stress comes from working hard.

I don't think that's true.

I've met people who work incredibly hard and seem energized by it. I've also met people carrying far less responsibility who are completely overwhelmed.

The more goals you try to accomplish in a fixed amount of time, the greater the tension becomes.

The Trap of Ambition

When I was a young band director, I had big dreams.

  • Superior ratings
  • A larger band
  • More Solo & Ensemble students
  • An award-winning jazz band
  • Every student to succeed
  • More beginners recruited
  • More money raised
  • Every instrument fixed

None of those goals were bad. In fact, most were noble.

The problem wasn't the goals. The problem was the denominator.

There were only so many hours in the day. The numerator kept growing while the denominator stayed the same. Eventually, the tension became impossible to ignore.

The Same Problem in Business

Years later, I discovered that leaving education didn't magically solve the problem. The formula followed me.

Today I can easily find myself pursuing fundraising growth, apparel sales, software development, marketing campaigns, new partnerships, AI tools, speaking opportunities, writing projects, and family commitments — all at once.

Again, none of these are bad goals. But every goal adds weight. Every opportunity becomes another backpack to carry. At some point, the issue isn't whether the opportunity is good — it's whether there is enough time to support it.

Two Ways to Reduce Tension

Most people try to solve tension by working harder. But the formula suggests there are really only two solutions.

Solution One

Increase the Denominator — create more usable time

  • Delegation
  • Automation
  • Hiring help
  • Extending deadlines
  • Improving systems
  • Saying "not yet"

Notice I didn't say "work longer hours." Working longer hours eventually reaches a limit. Systems scale better than exhaustion.

Solution Two

Reduce the Numerator — choose fewer goals

  • Every goal feels important — choose anyway
  • Every opportunity looks promising — filter anyway
  • Every project has potential — subtract anyway

Maturity often looks less like adding and more like subtracting. The most successful people I've met are not always the smartest or most talented. They are often the most disciplined about what they choose not to pursue.

The Bow and Arrow

Balance goals and time — the arrow flies

The bow represents your goals. The string represents time.

If the string is too short, the tension becomes overwhelming and something breaks. If the string is too loose, there isn't enough tension to move anything forward. But when goals and time are balanced correctly, the arrow flies.

The objective isn't to eliminate tension. It's to create the right amount of tension — enough to move forward, not so much that you snap.

Today's Question

Is the problem really the goal — or have I simply run out of denominator?

Sometimes the answer is to create more time. Sometimes the answer is to let go of a goal. Both require courage.

Life becomes much more manageable when we stop measuring ourselves by how many goals we can collect and start measuring ourselves by how intentionally we invest our time.

Because in the end, every goal costs time. And time is the one resource we never get back.
